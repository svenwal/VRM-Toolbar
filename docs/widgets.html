Widgets

Glance at your system from Notification Center or the desktop.

Adding a widget

  1. Start VRM Toolbar at least once, so macOS discovers its widgets.
  2. Click the clock in the menu bar, scroll down and choose Edit Widgets. On macOS 14+ you can also right-click the desktop.
  3. Search for VRM Toolbar, pick a widget and size, and add it.

Available widgets

WidgetSizesShows
BatterySmall, MediumA state-of-charge ring in the app’s green, amber and red. The medium size adds up to three of your other values.
ValuesSmall, Medium, LargeThe first 3, 4 or 8 values from your Displays, in the same order as the list, including each display’s label and unit.

Both use the dark, glowing look of the app, and the glow follows your charge level.

How fresh is the data?

The app hands its current values to the widgets and asks macOS to redraw them, at most about every 30 seconds. macOS decides when a widget is really redrawn, so it can lag behind the live menu bar. The time in the corner of the widget shows when the app last received data, and turns orange when the data is stale.

Widgets only update while VRM Toolbar is running, so enable Launch at login. Without the app, they keep showing the last values.

Which values appear?

Widgets use the displays you already set up, no extra configuration. Displays hidden by Only show while active are hidden in widgets too. To change what a widget shows, reorder your displays.

Under the hood

Widget extensions are sandboxed and have no network access, so the app writes a small snapshot file into a shared app-group container. Your VRM credentials never leave the app.

Self-built appWidgets need the app to be code-signed. Build with an Apple Development identity (see Build from source), and keep the app in a fixed place such as Applications.
